Used this way, AI supports revision and communication. It does not replace the writer’s

Writing Across School Subjects 
One useful thing about AI-supported writing is that it can help students communicate 
clearly in many different classes. 
In English, students may use it for essay development or revising analysis. 
In history, they may use it to organize arguments or sharpen explanations of evidence. 
In science, they may use it to clarify lab report language or explain findings more clearly. 
In computer science, they may use it to describe technical ideas in writing that others 
can understand. 
In presentations across subjects, they may use it to turn notes into clearer speaking 
points. 
This is why AI belongs in a student guide like this one. It can support writing, 
communication, and the clear presentation of ideas across school subjects. 
Student Example 
A student named Ava is writing a persuasive essay on whether schools should limit 
student phone use during class. 
She already has a general opinion, but she is not sure how to structure her argument. She 
asks AI: 
Help me brainstorm three possible arguments for limiting student phone use during 
class. 
The AI gives her several possibilities. She chooses the ones that make the most sense to 
her and then asks: 
Create a simple essay outline based on these ideas. 
After writing her first draft, she asks: 
Review this introduction and suggest how I can make it clearer and more engaging. 
In this example, Ava is still doing the thinking. She is choosing the position, writing the 
draft, and deciding what revisions to make. AI is helping her organize and improve her 
work, but it is not replacing her voice. 
That is the right balance.
