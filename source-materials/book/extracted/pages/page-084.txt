Student Example: Learning From the Second Try 
A student named Marcus is learning systems of equations. He understands the general 
idea, but one homework problem keeps going wrong. Instead of asking for the answer 
immediately, he asks AI: 
Show me how to solve this system step by step and explain why substitution 
works here. 
After reading the explanation, Marcus tries a second problem alone. Then he asks: 
Give me one more problem like this and check my work after I try it. 
In this example, AI helps Marcus understand the method and practice it independently. 
That is much more valuable than simply seeing the finished answer once. 
 
Classroom Idea 
Teachers can ask students to compare three kinds of math prompts: 
 an answer-only prompt 
 a step-by-step explanation prompt 
 a practice-and-feedback prompt 
Students can discuss which prompt leads to the strongest learning and why. This helps 
them understand that how they ask for help affects what kind of help they receive. 
Try This 
Use the prompt below and then work through the follow-up problem carefully on 
your own. The goal is not just to watch the process, but to notice whether you can 
apply the reasoning independently afterward. 
Prompt Example 
Show me how to solve this problem step by step, explain why each step works, and 
then give me one similar problem to try by myself. 
After using the prompt, ask yourself: 
 Did I understand the reasoning, or did I only follow the steps? 
 Could I solve a similar problem without help? 
 What kind of mistake am I most likely to make next time?
