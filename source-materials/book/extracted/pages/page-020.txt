This is why AI works best when it does more than explain. It should also help students 
rehearse, test, revise, and strengthen knowledge. 
For example, after receiving an explanation of the water cycle, a student could ask: 
Create five quiz questions about the water cycle. 
Then: 
Explain any answers I get wrong. 
That turns AI from an answer source into a learning system. 
 explanation 
 practice 
 feedback 
 correction 
 improvement 
A Student Example 
Imagine a student named Elena who is preparing for a test on cellular respiration. 
At first, the topic feels overwhelming. The textbook explanation is dense, and her notes 
are full of terms she does not fully understand. 
In this example, AI helped Elena do three important things: 
1. understand the idea 
2. connect it to something familiar 
3. practice and correct mistakes 
Teachers can use this chapter to help students distinguish between AI as a shortcut and 
AI as a support tool. 
One classroom activity could involve giving students the same topic and asking them to 
generate two types of prompts: 
 one prompt that only asks for an answer 
 one prompt that supports actual learning 
Learning-focused prompt: 
Explain the three main causes of World War I in simple terms for a high school student 
and then ask me three quiz questions. 
Students can compare the results and discuss which prompt better supports 
understanding.
