Students should confirm important information using: 
 textbooks 
 teacher materials 
 library resources 
 academic databases 
 trusted educational websites 
A good rule is this: 
Use AI to begin, explore, and organize research. Use trusted sources to confirm 
and support it. 
This approach allows students to benefit from AI’s speed and flexibility without treating 
it as the final source of truth. 
AI can support critical thinking, but only if students remain actively engaged in the 
process. 
Critical thinking means asking questions such as: 
 Is this explanation accurate? 
 What may be missing here? 
 Does this match what my class materials say? 
 Is there another perspective I should consider? 
 What sources can confirm this information? 
These are not just research skills. They are lifelong thinking skills. 
Students who use AI responsibly do not simply accept the first answer they receive. 
They examine it, question it, compare it, and improve on it. That is the kind of mindset 
that makes AI useful in education. 
Student Example 
A student named Caleb is preparing a short presentation on the Great Depression. He 
begins by asking AI for a simple overview of the main causes, then follows up with 
questions about how the economic collapse affected ordinary families and what research 
directions might make for a stronger presentation. After that, he checks his textbook 
and teacher notes to confirm the key details before building the final project. In this 
example, AI helps him begin quickly, organize his thinking, and generate better 
questions, but the final understanding still depends on his own judgment and 
verification.
