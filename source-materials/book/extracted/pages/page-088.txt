AI and Historical Simulations 
AI can also support interactive history learning through simulations. A student might 
ask AI to respond as if it were a historical figure from a certain time period, which can 
make learning more engaging and help history feel like lived experience rather than 
distant information. For example, a student could ask the tool to respond as a factory 
worker during the Industrial Revolution and then use that exchange to think more 
carefully about daily life, perspective, and historical context. 
For example, a student could ask: 
Act as a factory worker during the Industrial Revolution and answer my 
questions about daily life. 
This kind of prompt can be useful for perspective-taking and curiosity. But students 
should remember that the response is still a modern AI-generated interpretation, not a 
real primary source. Simulations are tools for exploration, not proof. 
What Students Must Verify 
History and social studies require verification because errors can change meaning 
significantly. 
Students should be especially careful with: 
 dates and timelines 
 quotations 
 statistics 
 primary source claims 
 descriptions of causes and effects 
AI can produce a clear explanation that still leaves out key details or blends facts 
incorrectly. That is why students should compare AI responses with textbooks, class 
notes, teacher guidance, and reliable historical sources.
