The response may provide a basic structure: 
 introduction 
 main claim 
 supporting points 
 counterargument 
 conclusion 
That kind of outline can be helpful because it gives students a framework to build on. 
But the student still needs to evaluate and adapt it. The outline should be a guide, not a 
script. 
Strong writing still depends on student judgment. Students must decide which ideas 
matter most, which examples fit best, and how they want to present their own thinking. 
Strengthening Writing Without Replacing It 
AI can also support revision. 
This may be one of its most useful roles in writing. Students often have a draft, but they 
are not sure how to make it stronger. They may know the writing feels weak in some 
way, but they cannot yet identify whether the problem is clarity, organization, repetition, 
or weak evidence. 
In those situations, AI can act like a feedback partner. 
For example, a student might ask: 
Review this paragraph and suggest ways to improve clarity. 
Or: 
Tell me how I can make this argument stronger without changing my main idea. 
These are strong uses of AI because they keep the student’s writing at the center. The 
AI is not replacing the writer. It is helping the writer revise more effectively. 
This approach is very different from asking: 
Write my essay about renewable energy. 
The second prompt turns AI into a substitute. The first prompts turn AI into a tool for 
improvement. 
That difference matters.
