Irresponsible uses include: 
 sharing private health information 
 using AI instead of trusted adults for serious concerns 
 following unverified fitness or nutrition advice blindly 
 treating AI as a doctor, therapist, or emergency response tool 
A good rule is this: Use AI for learning and planning, but use trusted people for 
care, safety, and real support. 
Student Example 
A student named Leila is working on a health class project about stress management. 
She understands the basic topic, but she wants help organizing her ideas. 
She asks AI: 
Explain three healthy ways students can manage stress at school and why each 
one helps. 
Then she asks: 
Give me five reflection questions I could answer about stress and focus in my 
own life. 
AI helps Leila understand the topic and think more clearly about her project, but she 
still uses class materials and her teacher’s instructions to complete the assignment. 
Classroom Idea 
Teachers can ask students to review several sample prompts and decide which ones are 
appropriate for AI and which should be taken to a trusted adult instead. For example: 
 Explain the benefits of stretching before exercise. 
 Help me plan a healthier sleep routine for school nights. 
 Tell me what to do about a serious injury. 
 Give me reflection questions about managing screen time. 
This helps students understand both the value and the limits of AI in wellness-related 
topics.
