Student Example 
A student named Riley needs to create a presentation for class on the future of 
transportation. 
At first, Riley’s only idea is “cars in the future,” which feels too broad. Riley asks AI: 
Give me five creative presentation ideas about the future of transportation for a high 
school student. 
The AI suggests several possibilities, including self-driving cars, flying taxis, smart public 
transportation, and environmentally friendly transit systems. Riley chooses the idea 
about future city transportation and then asks: 
Turn this into a presentation outline with three main sections. 
After that, Riley asks: 
Suggest two visual ideas that would make this presentation more interesting. 
In this example, AI helped Riley brainstorm, narrow the focus, organize the content, and 
improve the presentation design. But Riley still made the key creative choices. 
That is exactly the kind of balance students should aim for. 
Classroom Idea 
Teachers can use AI in brainstorming activities by asking students to compare AI-
generated ideas with their own. 
For example, a class could begin with a topic like “future schools” or “clean energy.” 
Students first brainstorm ideas on paper by themselves. Then they ask AI for additional 
possibilities. Finally, they compare the two sets of ideas and discuss: 
 Which AI ideas are useful? 
 Which ideas feel too generic? 
 What human ideas are more original? 
 How can AI suggestions be improved or combined with student thinking? 
This helps students see AI as a source of inspiration, not as a replacement for 
imagination.
