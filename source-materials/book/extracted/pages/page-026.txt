How Students Can Improve Their Prompts 
You do not need to write the perfect prompt on your first try. Strong AI users often 
improve prompts as they go. A student might begin with a basic question, realize the 
answer is too broad, and ask again with more detail. That is not failure. It is refinement. 
Student Example 
A student preparing for a quiz on the American Revolution first asks, “Tell me about 
the American Revolution.” The answer is broad and not especially helpful. They revise it 
to: “Explain the three main causes of the American Revolution in simple terms for a 
high school student and end with three quiz questions.” The response becomes more 
focused and directly useful for studying. 
Classroom Idea 
Teachers can give students the same weak prompt and ask them to improve it in pairs or 
small groups. This helps students see that prompt writing is really about precision, 
purpose, and communication.
