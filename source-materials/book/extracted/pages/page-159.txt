Deep Learning 
A type of machine learning that uses layered systems called neural networks to recognize 
complex patterns in large amounts of data. 
Digital Literacy 
The ability to use digital tools effectively and responsibly. AI literacy is becoming an 
increasingly important part of digital literacy. 
Digital Tools 
Software or online platforms used to complete tasks such as writing, designing, 
organizing, studying, or communicating. 
Ethics 
Principles that guide responsible behavior and decision-making. In AI, ethics includes 
questions of fairness, honesty, privacy, and responsible use. 
Feedback Loop 
A cycle in which information about performance is used to improve the next attempt. 
AI can support learning by helping students work through feedback loops. 
Generative AI 
A type of AI that creates new content such as text, images, code, music, or video based 
on patterns it has learned from training data. 
Hallucination 
An AI-generated response that sounds believable but is inaccurate, misleading, or 
completely made up. Hallucinations are one reason students must verify important 
information. 
Human Judgment 
A person’s ability to evaluate information, consider context, weigh consequences, and 
make thoughtful decisions. Human judgment remains essential when using AI. 
Input 
The information given to a system. In AI, input may include prompts, uploaded text, 
questions, or instructions.
