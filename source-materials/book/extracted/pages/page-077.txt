Students often encounter difficult texts in ELA, especially when reading older literature, 
unfamiliar vocabulary, or complex nonfiction. AI can be useful in these situations when 
students need help understanding the language or structure of a text. 
For example, a student reading Shakespeare might ask AI to paraphrase a difficult 
passage in modern language. A student reading a challenging article might ask for a short 
summary of the central claim. A student preparing for a discussion might ask for a list of 
major themes to watch for while reading. 
These are strong uses of AI because they support comprehension. The student is still 
doing the reading and thinking, but the tool helps remove some of the confusion that 
might block understanding. 
Students should be careful, however, not to let summaries replace reading. Reading a 
summary of a chapter is not the same as reading the chapter itself. AI can help students 
enter the text more confidently, but it should not become a substitute for engaging with 
the actual words on the page. 
AI for Brainstorming and Prewriting 
Many students struggle most at the beginning of a writing task. They may understand 
the assignment but feel stuck when trying to generate a thesis, narrow a topic, or decide 
what to say first. AI can be very helpful during this early stage. 
Help me brainstorm three possible themes in this novel that I could write about. 
A student preparing an argument essay, for example, might ask: 
Give me two possible positions on this issue and a counterargument for each 
one. 
These prompts do not ask AI to write the essay. They help the student generate 
possibilities. That difference is important. Strong prewriting support can help students 
get unstuck without taking ownership away from them.
