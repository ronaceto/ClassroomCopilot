Used poorly, however, it can make students overdependent. That is why it should be 
treated as a coach, not as a replacement for understanding. 
Choosing the Right Tool 
One of the most important AI skills students can develop is learning how to choose the 
right tool for the task. 
A student who needs help understanding a difficult topic may want an AI chat assistant. 
A student beginning a research project may want a research-focused tool. 
A student revising an essay may want a writing assistant. 
A student creating a presentation may want a design-oriented tool. 
A student learning Python may want a coding support tool. 
This is why the book includes diagrams such as The AI Landscape and Choosing the 
Right AI Tool. They help students see that “AI” is not just one thing. It is an 
ecosystem of different tools with different strengths. 
Students do not need to master every tool. They do need to become thoughtful about 
choosing the right one. 
Student Example 
A student named Ethan is working on three different tasks in the same week: 
 a history research project 
 a persuasive essay 
 a science presentation 
Instead of using the same AI tool in the same way for every task, Ethan chooses based 
on the need. 
For the history project, he uses a research-oriented tool to explore source-based 
background information. 
For the essay, he uses a writing tool to improve clarity in his draft. 
For the science presentation, he uses a design tool to help create clearer visuals. 
This is a good example of AI literacy in action. Ethan is not just “using AI.” He is 
choosing tools intentionally.
