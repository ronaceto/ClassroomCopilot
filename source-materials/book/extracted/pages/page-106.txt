When a topic has multiple perspectives, responsible AI use means asking broader 
questions, comparing responses, and checking additional sources rather than accepting 
one answer too quickly. 
This is another reason human judgment matters so much. AI can help students explore 
ideas, but students must still evaluate fairness, accuracy, and perspective. 
Privacy and Personal Information 
Responsibility also includes being careful about what information students share with AI 
tools. 
Students should avoid entering: 
 passwords 
 private school account information 
 personal identifying details 
 sensitive health information 
 confidential school documents 
 other students’ private information 
Even when an AI tool feels conversational, it is still a digital system. Students should use 
common sense and protect their privacy just as they would online in any other context. 
A good rule is: 
If you would not post it publicly or share it with a stranger, do not put it into an 
AI tool. 
That habit protects students and helps them build strong digital citizenship skills.
