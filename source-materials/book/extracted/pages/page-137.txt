Those habits matter in school, but they matter just as much in adult life. 
Student Example 
A student named Maya began using AI mostly to help with homework. 
At first, she used it to explain science concepts and generate quiz questions. Over time, 
she became more comfortable asking follow-up questions, checking explanations against 
class notes, and using AI to brainstorm project ideas. 
Later, Maya used AI to explore a possible future career in engineering. She asked about 
the skills engineers need, what kinds of projects they work on, and which classes might 
help prepare her. 
In each of these situations, AI helped Maya move forward. But the reason it was 
valuable is that Maya remained actively involved. She kept asking questions, checking 
information, and deciding what to do with the answers. 
That is what made her an AI-empowered learner. 
Classroom Idea 
Teachers can use this final chapter as a reflection activity. 
Students can be asked to write or discuss: 
 What is one responsible way I can use AI in school? 
 What is one way AI could help me learn more effectively? 
 What is one risk I need to watch out for when using AI? 
 How do I want to use AI differently after reading this book? 
This kind of reflection helps students move from information to ownership. It 
encourages them to think not just about what AI can do, but about how they want to 
use it.
