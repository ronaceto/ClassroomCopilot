This is one of the best uses of AI in creativity: not replacing the creator, but helping the

Creativity Across Subjects 
Students often assume brainstorming belongs only in creative writing or art, but AI-
supported creativity can be useful in many school settings. 
In English, students might use AI to develop story concepts, essay topics, or alternate 
interpretations of a text. 
In history, they might use AI to imagine a classroom debate, design historical interview 
questions, or explore what daily life was like in a certain era. 
In science, they might brainstorm experiment variations, project ideas, or real-world 
applications of a concept. 
In technology or computer science, they might generate app ideas, project themes, or 
different user features. 
In presentations, they might ask for different ways to explain a topic visually or 
creatively. 
This broader understanding is important because it shows students that creativity is not 
separate from academic success. It is part of it. 
AI as a Creativity Partner, Not a Creativity Replacement 
This distinction matters just as much in creativity as it does in writing or coding. 
AI can suggest. 
AI can expand. 
AI can reorganize. 
AI can inspire. 
But AI should not become the student’s imagination. 
A student who asks AI for an idea and then reshapes it, personalizes it, and develops it 
into something original is using the tool well. A student who simply accepts the first 
output and presents it as finished work is missing the chance to think creatively. 
Strong creative work still depends on human judgment. 
Students must decide: 
 Which idea feels strongest? 
 Which version is most interesting? 
 What should be added, changed, or removed? 
 What makes the work feel original?
