The Skills That Will Matter More 
As AI becomes more capable, some human skills become even more important. 
Students often assume that if AI can generate writing, solve problems, or answer 
questions, then human thinking matters less. In reality, the opposite is often true. 
When information becomes easier to generate, it becomes more important to evaluate, 
interpret, and apply that information wisely. 
That means the future will still strongly reward skills such as: 
 critical thinking 
 communication 
 creativity 
 collaboration 
 ethical judgment 
 adaptability 
 problem solving 
AI can help produce information quickly, but it cannot replace human responsibility, 
empathy, or wisdom. It cannot fully replace the value of asking the right question, 
choosing between competing ideas, or making a thoughtful decision in a complex 
situation. 
Students who develop these human strengths alongside AI literacy will be much better 
prepared for future careers than students who only learn how to use tools without 
learning how to think. 
AI Literacy as a Career Skill 
Just as digital literacy became essential in the internet age, AI literacy is becoming 
increasingly important in the AI age. 
AI literacy does not mean students need to become software engineers or machine 
learning experts. It means they should know enough to: 
 understand what AI can and cannot do 
 use AI tools effectively 
 write clear prompts 
 verify AI-generated information 
 think critically about outputs 
 use AI responsibly in school and work 
These are practical skills. 
A student who knows how to use AI to organize ideas, improve communication, 
research topics, and solve problems will likely have an advantage over someone who 
either avoids AI completely or uses it carelessly.
