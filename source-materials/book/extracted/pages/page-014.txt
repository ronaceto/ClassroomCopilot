Prediction, Not Understanding 
AI generates responses by prediction, not by understanding. 
When you type a prompt into an AI system, it does not pause and think like a person 
would. It does not reflect on its own experiences or reason through the meaning in the 
same way a human mind does. Instead, it predicts what words are most likely to come 
next based on the patterns it has learned. 
By predicting one word at a time, and then the next, and then the next, AI can build 
long responses that sound fluent and useful. This process happens very quickly, which is 
why AI tools can respond in seconds. 
A good way to think about it is this: AI is extremely good at generating language that 
sounds right, but that is different from actually knowing whether the content is true.  
That difference matters. 
Why AI Sometimes Makes Mistakes 
Because AI works by prediction, it can sometimes produce responses that sound correct 
but are actually wrong. 
An AI hallucination happens when the system generates false, inaccurate, or invented 
information in a confident tone. For example, an AI tool might: 
 give an incorrect date for a historical event 
 invent a quotation that never existed 
 describe a scientific process inaccurately 
 create a fake source or reference 
This can be dangerous if students assume the response must be correct simply because it 
sounds polished. 
The problem is not that AI is useless. The problem is that AI is not designed to care 
about truth in the way humans do. Its goal is to generate a response that fits the 
language patterns of the prompt. Sometimes that response is excellent. Sometimes it is 
flawed. 
That is why students must always bring their own judgment to the process.
