AI Is Not a Search Engine 
Many students compare AI tools to search engines, but the two are not the same.  A 
search engine usually finds existing information and links you to sources. An AI tool 
generates a brand-new response based on patterns in data. That makes AI feel more 
conversational, but it also means you must be more careful.  When you use a search 
engine, you can inspect the websites it found. When you use AI, you are often receiving 
a polished answer without immediately seeing where the information came from. 
AI is often best used for: 
 explanations 
 summaries 
 brainstorming 
 practice questions 
 idea generation 
Search engines and reliable sources are still essential for checking facts, verifying claims, 
and doing formal research. 
A Student Example 
A student named Jordan asks AI to explain the main causes of World War I. The 
response sounds clear and organized, but instead of trusting it immediately, Jordan 
checks it against class notes and a textbook. The explanation is mostly correct, but one 
part oversimplifies the role of alliances and leaves out an important detail discussed in 
class. By checking the response, Jordan gets the benefit of a quick explanation while still 
relying on trusted sources for accuracy. This is exactly how students should think about 
AI: it can be a useful starting point, but it is not the final authority. 
Classroom Idea 
Teachers can help students understand how AI works by comparing AI-generated 
responses to textbook explanations. 
For example, students could ask AI to explain a topic such as photosynthesis, the Civil 
War, or linear equations. Then they could compare that explanation with what appears 
in their class materials. 
This activity helps students see that AI can be useful, but it also helps them practice 
identifying missing details, oversimplifications, and possible inaccuracies.
