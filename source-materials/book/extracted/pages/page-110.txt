Chapter 18 — AI, Media Literacy, and What to 
Trust 
 
Key Question 
How can students tell what to trust in a world where AI can generate realistic text, 
images, audio, and video that may be misleading or false? 
Learning Objectives 
 Understand how AI can be used to create misinformation and manipulated 
media. 
 Recognize why believable content is not always trustworthy content. 
 Learn practical habits for checking claims, images, summaries, and viral 
posts. 
 Distinguish between healthy skepticism and automatic cynicism. 
 Use media literacy skills to verify what is real before sharing, trusting, or 
repeating it. 
Introduction 
Students today live in a media environment where information moves fast and attention 
is valuable. A post, image, video clip, or summary can spread across social platforms in 
minutes. In that environment, artificial intelligence makes both helpful and harmful 
things easier to create. AI can help people explain ideas, make visuals, and communicate 
more quickly. But it can also be used to generate misinformation, manipulate media, and 
create content that looks real even when it is false or misleading. That means students 
need a skill that goes beyond simply using AI tools: they need to know how to evaluate 
what they see and decide what deserves trust. 
AI media literacy means learning how to pause, question, verify, and think critically 
when digital content looks convincing. It includes checking sources, comparing claims, 
questioning motives, and recognizing that even realistic-looking images, videos, and 
summaries may not tell the truth. In a world shaped by synthetic media, strong students 
do not trust content just because it is polished. They ask better questions first.
